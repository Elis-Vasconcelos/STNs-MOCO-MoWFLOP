# `sharing/pareto_front_vs_cec/`

Correctness check of **this work's** wind-corrected MoWFLOP runs against the
**CEC 2025 / wflopcec26** reference, for the 10 Cazzaro & Pisinger "New
Sites" instances that CEC also ran.

Generated by `scripts/mowflop_comparison/pareto_check.py` (see that
directory's `README.md` for data sources, naming conventions, and how to
re-run). These are curated copies; the script's own scratch output is the
gitignored `plots/pareto_check/`.

## Files

- `pareto_ns<ID>_<algo>_p100_i50.png` — 20 figures (10 instances ×
  MOEA/D, NSGA-II). Each is a 2×5 grid of the **run-matched** comparisons:
  panel *k* overlays our run *k* Pareto front against CEC's matching run,
  both under the identical `(angle, wind)` draw. Comparison is run-matched
  rather than pooled because wind varies per run (power ~ wind³) and we
  have 10 runs per instance vs CEC's 20 — pooling would compare wind
  distributions, not algorithms.
- `pareto_check_summary_p100_i50.csv` — 200 rows (instance × algo × run):
  front sizes, front extremes on each side, their signed relative gaps
  (`d_cost_min_pct`, `d_power_max_pct`), and set-coverage `C(CEC, ours)` /
  `C(ours, CEC)`.

## Result

Across all 10 instances and both algorithms, the mean relative gap at the
front extremes is within **±0.1 %** (`ns48` matches CEC's power extreme to
`0.000000` on both algorithms). Set coverage sits in `0.2–0.9` in both
directions with neither side systematically at 1 — the expected signature
of two well-converged stochastic fronts interleaving along the same
frontier. Conclusion: once the per-run wind is matched, our executions
reproduce the CEC benchmark; the earlier divergence was the wind protocol,
not a bug.

| pool | instances | matched runs |
|---|---|---|
| `p100_i50` | ns41 ns48 ns101 ns178 ns192 ns202 ns203 ns440 ns465 ns488 | 10 (our `run_id` 0–9 ↔ CEC dirs 1–10) |

Instances without a CEC counterpart (sparse `*_r1e-04` / `*_r1e-05`,
`506–513`) are not covered here — they need an internally-pooled reference
set instead.
